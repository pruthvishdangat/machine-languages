Artist Residency Program

Embedded practice in active industrial sites

Our residency program places artists, researchers, and practitioners in sustained collaboration with Chicago's working industrial facilities. This isn't about romanticizing labor or extracting material—it's about developing aesthetic practices grounded in real sites, real work, and accountable relationships.

Program Overview

Three months of attention

Residents spend twelve weeks embedded in an active industrial site—warehouse, fabrication shop, distribution center, or manufacturing facility. The residency prioritizes observation, documentation, and dialogue over predetermined outcomes.

What we provide

Site Access

Ongoing access to an industrial facility, arranged through our network of partner sites. You'll work alongside employees, with schedules negotiated to respect operational needs.

Stipend

$6,000 to support three months of research and material exploration. Additional funds available for documentation, materials, or fabrication costs.

Studio Space

Desk and storage space in our Pilsen collective studio for the duration of the residency, plus access to basic tools and equipment.

Mentorship

Regular meetings with collective members, feedback sessions, and connection to Chicago's broader network of artists working with industrial materials and sites.

What we ask

Residents commit to:

  • Minimum three days per week at the host site
  • Monthly public presentations documenting the research process
  • Respectful, accountable engagement with site workers and management
  • Contribution to our collective archive (documentation, recordings, interviews, etc.)
  • Final presentation of work or research developed during residency

We don't require finished artworks. Some residents produce installations or performances; others compile archives, conduct interviews, or develop proposals for future projects. What matters is sustained, serious attention.

Philosophy

Residency as relationship, not extraction

Traditional artist residencies often treat sites as raw material—background for individual creative production. We're trying something different: residencies structured around reciprocity, accountability, and time.

This means:

Workers have agency

Site employees aren't subjects to be documented but collaborators who shape the residency's direction. Residents work with them, not on them. Workers retain rights to representation and veto power over documentation.

Time matters

Three months is barely enough to begin understanding a site's rhythms, cultures, and complexities. We prioritize slowness, boredom even—the kind of sustained attention that reveals patterns invisible to quick observation.

Context is everything

Industrial sites exist within specific economic, political, and social contexts. Good residency work attends to these structures rather than treating spaces as neutral aesthetic resources.

Process over product

We value research, documentation, conversation, and failed experiments as much as finished artworks. The residency itself is the work.

Partner Sites

Where residents work

We maintain ongoing relationships with several Chicago industrial facilities that host our residents. Sites rotate based on availability, operational needs, and project fit. Recent and current partners include:

South Side Warehousing & Distribution

Active logistics facility specializing in regional distribution. 200,000+ sq ft facility operating two shifts daily. Partners since 2021.

BRIDGEPORT

West Loop Fabrication Co.

Custom metalworking and fabrication shop. Small-scale production, emphasis on skilled craft. Family-owned, operating since 1983.

NEAR WEST SIDE

Pilsen Manufacturing Cooperative

Worker-owned facility producing component parts for construction and machinery. Focus on sustainable manufacturing practices.

PILSEN

Cicero Industrial Storage

Climate-controlled warehouse serving pharmaceutical and food industries. Highly regulated environment with complex logistics systems.

Past Residents

Previous residency projects

FALL 2025

Sound artist documenting shift changes

Three months recording the acoustic signature of transitions between first and second shift at a warehousing facility—conversations, preparation rituals, equipment handoffs. Resulted in a 40-minute composition premiered at Experimental Sound Studio.

SUMMER 2025

Choreographer translating assembly processes

Learned and documented the physical movements of metal fabrication—bending, welding, grinding. Developed these into performance scores exploring the intelligence of industrial gesture. Work shown at Links Hall.

SPRING 2025

Archivist collecting worker oral histories

Conducted extensive interviews with current and retired employees about changes in labor practices, technology, and workplace culture over decades. Archive donated to Chicago Labor and History Society.

WINTER 2024

Photographer documenting material failures

Systematic photography of broken, worn, and discarded industrial materials—cracked welds, stripped bolts, fatigued metal. Exploring failure as aesthetic category and evidence of use.

FALL 2024

Writer investigating break room cultures

Ethnographic observation and interviews focused on the social dynamics of workplace rest periods. Resulted in a published essay and ongoing research project on informal workplace organization.

How to Apply

Application process

Who should apply

We welcome applications from artists, researchers, writers, choreographers, archivists, and other practitioners whose work engages with labor, material culture, industrial processes, or site-specific practice.

We particularly encourage applications from:

  • Current or former industrial workers
  • Chicago-based artists of color
  • Working-class artists
  • Those new to institution environments

No advanced degree required. We care about curiosity, commitment, and the quality of your questions more than credentials.

Application materials

  • Brief statement of interest (500 words max)
  • Description of proposed research or project (500 words max)
  • Work samples (up to 10 images, 5 minutes video, or equivalent)
  • CV or resume
  • Optional: letter of reference

We read for questions, not answers. Tell us what you want to investigate and why it matters. Show us how you think.

Support

Program funding

Our residency program is supported by grants from the Joyce Foundation, the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, the Illinois Arts Council Agency, and individual donors committed to supporting artist-worker collaborations.

Partner sites donate space and access. Collective members volunteer time for mentorship and coordination. This makes the residency possible but also keeps it small—we can only support 4-6 residents per year.
