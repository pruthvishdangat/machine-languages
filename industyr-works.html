Selected Works

Projects 2019–2026

Our work takes the form of installations, performances, publications, and long-term research collaborations. Each project emerges from sustained engagement with a specific site, material, or system.

Current

Shift Patterns

2026 | Sound Installation | 8-channel audio, 24-hour loop

A sonic architecture constructed from 24 hours of continuous recording inside an active South Side warehouse. Eight speakers positioned throughout the gallery emit overlapping soundscapes—forklift hydraulics, conveyor rhythms, break room microwaves, the specific quality of silence at 3am when machinery rests.

The piece reveals temporal patterns invisible to those working within them: how sound accumulates and disperses, how industrial time differs from clock time, how labor creates its own cyclical structures that never quite repeat.

Commissioned by the Renaissance Society. Presented in collaboration with workers from the documented facility, who participated in selection and editing of the final composition.

On view through March 2026 | The Renaissance Society, University of Chicago

2025

Glove Portraits

2025 | Installation | 200 work gloves, photographs, data prints

An archive of 200 pairs of work gloves donated by employees of a closing West Side warehouse. Each glove photographed in standardized lighting, catalogued with worker tenure, hand dominance, primary tasks, and detailed notes on wear patterns—reinforced fingertips, frayed cuffs, oil stains, thermal discoloration.

The installation presents the gloves in a grid, each accompanied by its archival photograph and data sheet. Taken together, they form a kind of collective portrait—not of individual workers but of labor itself, made visible through the accumulated traces on materials designed to be disposable.

Museum of Contemporary Photography, Chicago

Factory Scores (Performance Series)

2025 | Performance | Five 90-minute performances

A series of performances translating industrial procedures into choreographic scores. Performers learned specific factory workflows—pallet loading, inventory counting, equipment maintenance—and executed them in gallery space with minute precision.

What happens when the logic of efficiency becomes aesthetic? When muscle memory developed for production is performed without product? The work makes visible the strange beauty of optimized motion and the physical intelligence embedded in supposedly "unskilled" labor.

Developed in collaboration with current and former warehouse workers who served as choreographic consultants.

Co-Prosperity Sphere, Bridgeport

Thermal Memory

2025 | Photography Series | 40 thermal images

Thermal imaging of surfaces in a shuttered Pilsen manufacturing facility, capturing residual heat signatures months after closure. Machinery retains warmth in unexpected patterns—friction points, motor housings, places where hands repeatedly gripped.

The images reveal that buildings remember. Heat persists as ghost, as evidence of use that outlasts intention. Industrial archaeology rendered visible in the infrared spectrum.

Published in limited edition monograph | Co-published with Green Lantern Press

2024

Break Room Dialogues

2024 | Publication & Exhibition | 300-page book, video installation

Two years of recorded conversations in factory break rooms across Chicago's South and West sides. Workers discussing everything from shift schedules to metaphysics, from union grievances to family recipes. The conversations are mundane and profound, boring and riveting—often simultaneously.

The publication presents transcripts alongside photographs of the spaces themselves: vending machines, microwave ovens, time clocks, bulletin boards. The exhibition included a reconstruction of a composite break room where visitors could listen to selected conversations.

All participants received copies of the book and editorial input on their sections. Proceeds support workers' centers in participating neighborhoods.

Exhibited at the Chicago Cultural Center | Published by Half Letter Press

Pallet Census

2024 | Ongoing Archive | Database, photographs, sculptural interventions

An ongoing project documenting shipping pallets across Chicago—their movements, markings, material composition, and eventual fate. Each photographed pallet is logged by type, condition, origin markers, and location found.

Pallets are infrastructure we've learned not to see, despite being one of the most ubiquitous industrial objects. They circulate globally, accumulate markings and damage, and encode information about supply chains, labor standards, and material economies.

The archive currently includes 1,200+ pallets. Selected examples are presented as sculptural objects in exhibition contexts.

Ongoing | Archive accessible by appointment

2023

Machine Genealogies

2023 | Research Project | Mixed media installation

Tracing the biography of a single industrial lathe from its 1967 manufacture in Ohio through five ownership changes, two facility closures, three cities, and eventual obsolescence. The project reconstructed this history through serial numbers, maintenance logs, auction records, and interviews with operators across decades.

The installation presented documentation alongside the machine itself (purchased at salvage auction), revealing how objects accumulate histories—not just wear and modification, but stories, expertise, and relationship to labor that can't be catalogued.

Luminary Arts Center, St. Louis (traveling exhibition)

Load/Unload

2023 | Video Installation | 4-hour loop, two channels

Parallel videos of loading dock activity at a distribution center: one camera fixed on workers loading a truck, the other on simultaneous unloading at a different bay. The work runs for four hours—a half shift—with no editing, no sound, no explanation.

Watching it requires endurance, boredom, attention. The same qualities required of the labor it documents. Patterns emerge slowly: rhythms of efficiency, moments of rest, the choreography of bodies working in practiced coordination.

Roots & Culture Contemporary Art Center, Chicago

2022

Inventory of Dust

2022 | Installation | Collected samples, microscopy, display cases

Industrial dust collected from fifteen different Chicago manufacturing and warehouse facilities, each sample analyzed for composition and displayed with microscope imagery revealing unexpected beauty—metal particulate, textile fibers, aggregate residue.

Dust is byproduct, waste, the thing cleaned away. But it's also evidence: each sample encodes what's made in that space, the materials handled, the processes executed. Under magnification, it becomes landscape.

Zhou B Art Center, Bridgeport

Time Card Translations

2022 | Publication | 100-page risograph-printed booklet

Two months of time cards from six workers at a South Chicago facility, reproduced at actual size alongside their reflections on what the cards record and what they obscure—gaps, overtime, the impossibility of documenting actual labor.

A meditation on how work is measured, how time is commodified, and what administrative documents simultaneously reveal and erase.

Published by Threewalls | Edition of 500

2021–2019

South Side Industrial Audio Archive

2019–2021 | Archive | 200+ hours of field recordings

The project that initiated the collective: two years of systematic audio documentation of South Side industrial sites. Recordings made both in active facilities (with worker permission) and abandoned buildings scheduled for demolition.

The archive captures soundscapes now largely vanished—particular machines, acoustic signatures of specific spaces, environmental ambiences. It's organized both chronologically and thematically, accessible to researchers and artists.

Archived at University of Chicago Special Collections | Selected works released as limited vinyl editions

Disappeared Factories

2020 | Photography & Mapping Project

Documentary photography of twenty industrial sites in various stages of closure, demolition, or conversion. Each site photographed monthly over one year to capture transformation.

Presented as large-scale prints alongside maps showing the spatial distribution of industrial decline across the South and West sides. The work refuses nostalgia, instead offering clear-eyed witness to economic transformation and its architectural traces.

Andrew Rafacz Gallery, West Loop
