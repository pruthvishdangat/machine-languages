"Shift Patterns" at the Renaissance Society

"industyr of the ordinary's latest installation transforms the gallery into something like a secular temple of labor—eight channels of warehouse acoustics creating an architecture of sound that's as rigorous as it is haunting. This isn't ambient music or documentary; it's evidence of time structured by capital, made visceral."

The Artists Mapping Chicago's Industrial Memory

"For five years, this South Side collective has been doing the kind of cultural work that feels increasingly rare: patient, collaborative, embedded in place. Their projects don't arrive from outside to 'activate' a neighborhood—they emerge from sustained attention to what's already happening."

Hyperallergic · November 2025

When Process Becomes Performance

"The collective's performance series at Co-Prosperity Sphere literalized something usually metaphorical: turning the gestures of factory labor into choreography. Watching five performers execute the movements of pallet loading for 90 minutes shouldn't be riveting, but it absolutely was—a meditation on muscle memory, efficiency, and the strange beauty of optimized motion."

Chicago Collective Wins Emerging Artists Grant

"The $50,000 grant from the Joyce Foundation will fund industyr of the ordinary's ambitious three-year project documenting and responding to the closing of a South Chicago steel distribution facility, working directly with displaced workers to create an archive and series of installations."

Mousse Magazine · June 2025

Objects Remember: Material Culture and Labor History

"Their installation at the Museum of Contemporary Photography—a grid of 200 work gloves worn by employees of a closing warehouse—is deceptively simple. Each glove is photographed and cataloged with details of wear patterns, the worker's tenure, and their preferred hand position for lifting. The result is simultaneously data set and portrait, archive and elegy."

Read feature →
BOMB Magazine · March 2025

In Conversation: Attention as Practice

"A wide-ranging discussion with collective members about time, boredom, and what it means to watch a place for months before making anything. 'The work isn't the installation,' one member explains. 'The work is the 200 hours sitting in a shipping yard. The installation is documentation.'"
